# Small-Artist Style Atlas — 小画师审美样本图谱

> Purpose: compare the creator references named by the user and distill reusable decisions into an independent portrait-design system. This is not a recipe to clone any single finished image. It transfers mechanisms, not exact characters, palettes, props or signatures.
>
> Target mode: refined female character portraits / half-body commissions, often on white or pale backgrounds. Keep it separate from cinematic posters and environment-led painterly scenes.

## 0. Evidence rules

- **Direct visual observation:** a specific public artwork image or artwork page is accessible.
- **Portfolio-level observation:** a creator gallery / multiple works are indexed, but not every image can be inspected full-size.
- **Search-only signal:** only an artwork title, process title or mention appears; evidence is insufficient for a confident style claim.
- Never turn a search-only or ambiguous identity into a permanent aesthetic rule. Direct user-provided samples override weak public-search inference.

## 2. Independent composite — the “小画师脑”

Do not average all six into one global filter. Synthesize a layered decision system:

### A. Face-first appeal
- Design eye shape, gaze, eyelid thickness, cheek color, mouth restraint and head tilt before clothing detail.
- Permit subtle asymmetry in eyes, catchlights, fringe and expression; avoid mirrored beauty-filter geometry.
- The character's temperament determines expression: playful, aloof, tender, tired, cool or quietly confident.

### B. Portrait macro-shapes
- Main shapes are head / hair, shoulder line, collar / neck bow and one sleeve / hand gesture.
- Use close crop and mild asymmetry. Hair, bows, sleeves or ribbons may cross the frame edge only when they strengthen the silhouette.
- Keep the face readable; white / pale negative space is an active composition element, not missing content to fill.

### C. Costume as an engineered miniature
- Build base garment → construction layer → ornament layer.
- Show seams, closures, gathers, cuffs and attachment points; lace follows an edge, ruffles attach to a seam, ribbon fastens something, jewelry hangs from a visible anchor.
- Use **2–3 controlled detail clusters** (often head / hair, collar / chest, sleeve / hand) while allowing broad fabric and skin areas to stay quiet.
- Repeated motifs change scale or placement; do not repeat identical trinkets until the outfit becomes noise.

### D. Crisp structure + selective tactile paint
- Keep face, hair silhouette and garment construction readable.
- Use hand-drawn line variation, limited hatching, local color transitions and slight edge loss to prevent sterile uniformity.
- Introduce semi-painterly treatment where it adds material: skin transitions, hair reflections, satin, glass or gemstones. Do not blur every edge or gloss every surface.

### E. Tiny focal interaction
- Give the hand a reason to exist: touch a collar, adjust a glove, hold a charm, pull a ribbon taut or gather a sleeve.
- The hand / prop / jewelry creates a secondary focal cluster; its color echoes the face or costume.
- Action must be visible in fingers, wrist angle and cloth response. Avoid static doll-hands and unrelated props.

### F. Color roles
- Assign jobs before choosing exact hues: pale base, structural dark, skin / hair warmth and one accent family.
- Let the character dictate the palette. Do not force every portrait into black-and-white Gothic Lolita, aqua pastels, teal-orange contrast or jewel colors.
- White cloth still needs value separation from a white background; use subtle cool / warm line variation and small local shadows to preserve volume.

### G. Human finish
- Vary edges: crisp around eyes, fingers, seams and jewelry; softer in selected hair / cloth edges; nearly lost where pale forms meet the white field.
- Keep detail uneven by design: face / hand get priority; secondary cloth panels stay calmer.
- Avoid plastic skin, equal sharpness everywhere, random sparkle, over-symmetric features and copy-pasted fold patterns.

## 3. Mode selection

- **Clean white-background portrait:** face + hair silhouette + garment construction + 2–3 detail clusters + quiet field.
- **Pastel accessory portrait:** same construction discipline, with a stronger hand / jewelry interaction and delicate transparent color.
- **Cel-painterly hybrid:** crisp base shapes with selective glossy / painterly accents.
- **Painterly narrative scene:** route to the separate painterly-story grammar; borrow only face / costume hierarchy.
- **Cinematic poster:** route to poster grammar; do not force giant graphic masses into a bust portrait.

Choose one primary mechanism and at most one secondary influence. Example: “clear cel-like shapes + a hand / accessory micro-story” is an intentional combination; “all signature motifs at once” is not.

## 4. NAI5 execution sequence

1. Choose the primary portrait mode.
2. Describe face / expression and hair silhouette concretely.
3. Decide crop, shoulder angle and hand gesture.
4. Design clothing layers and attachment logic.
5. Select 2–3 dense detail clusters and quiet zones.
6. Assign color roles and material-specific light response.
7. Add one secondary focal object only if it strengthens character / gesture.
8. Remove generic mood words and redundant synonyms; keep visible relationships.
9. Keep the user's fixed artist stack and quality layer external by default.

## 5. Acceptance test

Fail this “小画师脑” when the prompt reads as a generic pretty girl plus accessories; the face is an afterthought; frills have no construction; every surface is equally sharp / soft; the blank background becomes scenery; the hand has no interaction; or unverified creator identities are treated as facts.

Pass when face appeal, hairstyle, outfit silhouette and gesture already feel designed before rendering adjectives are added.
