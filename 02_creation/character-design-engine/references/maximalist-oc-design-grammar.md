# Maximalist OC Design Grammar — 极繁精美人设

> A dedicated character-design preset for the owner's preferred **female-oriented maximalist OC aesthetic**. This governs the character itself: silhouette, costume engineering, ornament systems, material layering, and visual rhythm. It is separate from the “giant close-up backdrop + headshots” presentation preset.

## Core direction

The target is **极繁精美人设**, not merely “many details.”

The character should look like a deliberately engineered collectible fashion character whose identity is readable through the **silhouette, costume architecture, ornament ecosystem, material hierarchy, and repeated motif language**.

The key feeling is:

> **A richly layered, highly recognizable fashion silhouette whose complexity rewards close inspection without becoming random decoration.**

When the user explicitly requests “极繁 / 极繁精美 / OC圈高完成度人设 / 华丽人设”, this preset takes priority over the normal complexity-reduction instinct.

## 1. Complexity must be structural

High complexity is desirable, but it must come from stacked design systems:

**large silhouette → garment architecture → signature extension → repeated motif system → dangling / floating micro-details → tiny decorative puncta.**

Do not produce “simple dress + many flowers.” Instead engineer multiple interacting layers:
- oversized head or shoulder silhouette;
- strong bodice/corset architecture;
- asymmetric sleeves, cape, bustle, apron, overskirt, train or side panels;
- repeated ornaments that share one visual language;
- long ribbons, cords, chains, tassels or fabric strips that extend the silhouette;
- secondary motifs embedded into hems, collars, gloves, stockings and shoes.

## 2. Exaggerated silhouette is preferred

The examples favor **large readable shapes** that make the character recognizable before micro-detail.

Useful silhouette moves:
- enormous bonnet / hat / crown / head ornament;
- unusually large sleeves;
- broad shoulder structures;
- oversized bows;
- long side drapes or tails;
- multi-tiered skirts with irregular projection;
- giant hair masses or highly graphic twin tails;
- cape / wing / petal / feather structures expanding beyond the body;
- one side substantially heavier than the other.

A maximalist character should still read in monochrome silhouette.

## 3. Layered couture construction

Use at least 3–5 visibly different garment layers when the brief calls for maximalism.

Preferred progression:

**skin / base → fitted structural layer → decorative textile layer → oversized extension → micro-ornament system.**

Examples:
- sheer blouse under corset under embroidered jacket under oversized cape;
- fitted bodice under petal-like overskirt under translucent side panels;
- underskirt + lace petticoat + contrasting outer skirt + long detached fabric panels;
- high collar + chest ornament + ribbon harness + waist jewelry.

Each layer needs a distinct cut, material or spatial role. Avoid several layers that all look like the same ruffle.

## 4. Ornament ecosystem

Accessories should form a **family**, not a shopping list.

Pick one dominant motif family:
- flowers / thorns / vines
- stars / celestial geometry
- shells / pearls / coral
- porcelain / ribbons / bows
- keys / clocks / filigree
- feathers / wings / birds
- insects / wings / botanical structures
- bells / charms / tassels
- bones / horns / claws
- sweets / fruit / confectionery

Then let that family recur at different scales:
**hair → face/neck → chest → waist → skirt → footwear → handheld prop.**

Variation in size is important: one oversized anchor, several medium repetitions, many tiny echoes.

## 5. Asymmetry and directional weight

Do not distribute complexity evenly.

Prefer:
- heavy ornament cluster on one side of the head;
- one oversized sleeve vs one fitted sleeve;
- one large shoulder element with the opposite side quiet;
- long ribbons or chains falling primarily from one side;
- asymmetric skirt panels;
- one leg visually heavier because of stocking, armor, lace or accessory density.

Complexity should create **directional weight** and guide the eye.

## 6. Material collision

Maximalist designs benefit from deliberate material contrast.

Useful pairings:
- translucent organza × velvet
- satin × matte woven fabric
- lace × leather
- pearls × metal filigree
- glossy enamel × rough textile
- porcelain × ribbon
- feathers × structured tailoring
- biological membrane × couture fabric

At least one material contrast should be structurally important, not merely mentioned.

## 7. Exposure should be designed

When appropriate, exposed skin can act as a **quiet counter-mass** against dense costume.

Prefer deliberate areas:
- shoulders / collarbone;
- upper back;
- side waist;
- thigh framed by asymmetric skirt openings;
- arms between gloves and sleeves.

The contrast should read as **skin → dense garment → translucent layer**, rather than indiscriminate revealing.

## 8. Hair is part of costume architecture

For maximalist OC design, hair should not be a generic “long hair” afterthought.

Use:
- giant curls as silhouette masses;
- ribbon-integrated braids;
- ornamental locks that mirror the costume motif;
- long tails acting like fabric;
- hair ornaments embedded into the hairstyle rather than floating on top;
- asymmetrical hair volume that counterbalances costume weight.

Hair + head ornament should often form the strongest upper silhouette.

## 9. Micro-detail hierarchy

Use three detail scales:

**Macro:** silhouette, skirt/sleeve/headpiece, large extension.  
**Meso:** corset seams, embroidery panels, bows, chains, lace bands, appliqué.  
**Micro:** beads, tiny charms, stitches, miniature flowers, buckles, dangling ornaments.

Do not spend all complexity at micro scale. The macro and meso layers must already feel luxurious.

## 10. Color hierarchy

Maximalism does not require many colors.

Prefer:
- pale field + one rich garment mass;
- 2–3 dominant colors + metallic neutral;
- one saturated punctum repeated in several strategic locations.

The same accent should recur across unrelated garment zones to unify the design.

Examples:
- ivory + dusty mauve + deep violet + silver;
- cream + jade + wine red + antique gold;
- white + powder blue + cobalt + pearl;
- black + ivory + teal + crimson.

Avoid local rainbow noise.

## 11. Face and identity remain readable

Extreme costume complexity must not erase the character.

Maintain a clean beauty hierarchy:

**eyes / expression → hair and head silhouette → signature motif → upper-body costume anchor → lower-body architecture → micro-details.**

Keep at least one readable skin or fabric field around the face.

## 12. Pose is part of the design

A maximalist outfit needs a pose that activates the architecture.

Preferred pose behaviors:
- three-quarter fashion stance with one leg crossing;
- one knee bent and skirt pulled outward;
- ribbon / cape / sleeve being deliberately manipulated;
- hand lifting veil, hat, sleeve, bouquet or accessory;
- torso twist creating opposing fabric flow;
- one arm raised to create a long vertical line;
- gentle crouch / dip when the skirt or cape can form a strong shape.

The pose should explain why large shapes flare, overlap, swing or fold.

## 13. “Expensive” details that often work

Useful high-value construction cues:
- layered pearl strands following garment seams;
- embroidered borders that repeat a motif;
- sculptural collars;
- detachable sleeves;
- corset panel geometry;
- translucent inset panels;
- miniature charms attached to major structures;
- tassels terminating long lines;
- tiny repeated flowers embedded in larger floral forms;
- jewelry integrated into clothing fasteners;
- oversized signature ornament balanced by restrained secondary pieces.

The objective is **designed density**, not maximum object count.

## 14. Anti-patterns

Avoid:
- simple base dress + random flowers/pearls pasted everywhere;
- every area equally ornate;
- three unrelated motif families;
- “gothic + angel + butterfly + clock + crystal” with no common grammar;
- decorative vocabulary with no relationship to silhouette;
- micro-detail without macro silhouette;
- overly clean generic anime school/fantasy clothing disguised with more accessories;
- symmetrical ornamentation by default;
- relying on generic words such as “luxurious, intricate, gorgeous, highly detailed” instead of concrete construction.

### Diagnostic

If removing 50% of the micro-decoration leaves essentially the same character, the design is not truly maximalist; it is merely accessorized.

A strong maximalist OC should lose **specific identity** when its major garment architecture or motif system is removed.

## 15. Difference from the presentation preset

Do not confuse this with the separate composition preset:

- **Maximalist OC Design Grammar** = how the character is engineered.
- **Close-Up Backdrop + Element Sticker Preset** = how the finished character is laid out in a showcase image.

They can be combined:
**極繁精美人设 + giant close-up backdrop + full-body foreground + headshot/element stickers.**
