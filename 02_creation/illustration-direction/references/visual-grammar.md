# Illustration Visual Grammar — 画面命题、视觉母题与叙事构图

> This reference encodes a visual-design layer for original illustration direction. It is derived from repeated review of high-end Pixiv / social-media illustration examples supplied by the owner, but does not imitate any specific artist. The goal is to make the image feel authored: one idea, one visual grammar, one readable moment.

## 1. 核心转向：从“漂亮角色”到“一个被设计过的画面”

An illustration is not a character plus a background. It is a **single visual proposition** in which character, environment, camera, light, negative space, objects, and color masses cooperate.

Before writing a scene, answer this sentence:

> **如果删掉人物和文字，这张图还剩下什么构图命题？**

Good answers are structural or narrative:
- a tiny figure trapped inside enormous architectural frames
- white hair becoming a river across a reflective surface
- an empty ceremonial space revealing that someone arrived too late
- repeated circular lights leading a figure into darkness
- birds, mirrors, doors, flowers, cables, windows, or waves physically carrying the eye toward the character

Weak answers are merely decorative:
- beautiful fantasy garden
- magical particles around a goddess
- glowing flowers behind a pretty girl
- detailed cathedral background

The first group creates a picture. The second creates a backdrop.

## 2. The Five-Layer Authored Image

Every narrative illustration should attempt to solve five layers before prompt compilation:

### A. Image Thesis
One sentence describing the visual idea, not the plot.

`A tiny white figure is framed repeatedly by enormous silver arches.`

### B. Captured Moment
A 0.5-second state with a before/after implication.

`She has just noticed the empty chair; one second later she will leave without touching it.`

### C. Visual Motif
One object, shape, material, or spatial behavior that repeats enough to become the composition's grammar.

Examples: arches, mirrors, birds, long ribbons, reflected light, staircases, window grids, water ripples, flower stems.

A motif is not decoration. It must **frame, divide, connect, conceal, or lead**.

### D. Environmental Agency
The environment must do something to the character or camera:
- frame her
- hide part of her
- force a perspective
- reflect her
- distort her silhouette
- create a scale contrast
- carry her motion
- preserve evidence of what happened

If the environment merely sits behind the character, it is under-designed.

### E. Narrative Residue
One concrete trace whose state implies an unseen event.

Examples: an empty chair still decorated with flowers, a half-open door, wet footprints that stop abruptly, a fallen glove, a torn ribbon caught on a railing, a reflection that does not quite match.

One strong residue is preferable to ten lore props.

## 3. Composition Should Be Built as Geometry

Do not start with "centered character". Start with large shapes.

Plan:
1. one dominant mass
2. one counter-mass or void
3. one directional flow
4. one focal interruption

Useful structures:
- diagonal bands crossing the frame
- nested arches or windows
- large circular/elliptical framing around a small figure
- vertical architectural repetition
- a sweeping organic line such as hair, cloth, smoke, branches, cables, or water
- foreground objects cutting through the frame
- a figure deliberately dwarfed by the environment

The eye should be able to read the image as 3–5 large shapes at thumbnail size.

## 4. Scale Contrast Is a Storytelling Tool

Use scale mismatch deliberately.

### Environment-dominant
Character ≤ 1/3 of the frame. Use when the world, loneliness, divinity, danger, or memory is the subject.

### Balanced
Character ≈ 1/2. Use when the character and environment have equal narrative importance.

### Character-dominant
Character ≥ 2/3. Use when expression, costume, gesture, or identity is the subject.

Do not default to 2/3 simply because the prompt is about an OC.

## 5. Occlusion Is Not an Error

High-end illustration frequently allows the environment to interrupt the character.

Use controlled occlusion:
- foreground frame covers part of hair or skirt
- flowers or furniture cut across the lower silhouette
- architectural beams pass behind or in front of the figure
- reflections duplicate or partially replace the body
- large environmental shapes crop the character at the edge

The character must remain identifiable, but **complete visibility is not the goal**.

Avoid accidental occlusion that destroys hands, face, or the defining silhouette.

## 6. Negative Space Must Have a Job

Empty space is not leftover canvas.

Assign it a function:
- directional breathing room toward the character's gaze
- scale contrast around a tiny figure
- a quiet field that makes one dense area hit harder
- visual silence before or after an action
- a surface for a single light gradient or shadow

Do not fill negative space with particles, petals, stars, lens flares, decorative symbols, or random props merely because it looks empty.

## 7. Density Rhythm

A good image has uneven density.

Use:
- **dense zone** — the visual event, usually motif + figure + important interaction
- **quiet zone** — large simple material field
- **transition path** — a line, shadow, reflection, repeated object, or value shift connecting them

Do not make every corner equally detailed. Equal detail everywhere produces catalog art rather than authored composition.

## 8. Color-Mass Design

Think in 3–4 major masses before local colors:

1. dominant field
2. character mass
3. structural counter-mass
4. one accent

Prefer a restrained palette with one meaningful contrast. The accent should support the visual thesis, not become generic "beautiful lighting".

Examples:
- white / silver / pale blue + one cyan accent
- warm cream / brown / muted red + one white subject
- deep blue / black + cold white figure
- ivory / pale gold + a single dark architectural mass

## 9. Light Must Explain the Composition

Use light to reveal the thesis.

The main light source should:
- originate from a visible or physically plausible direction
- create a readable value hierarchy
- leave at least one region intentionally quiet or dark when appropriate
- interact with the environment

A rim light is not a substitute for lighting design.

Prefer:
- window light that creates architectural bands
- reflected water light that connects figure and environment
- backlight that turns hair into a graphic shape
- a distant practical light sequence that creates perspective
- overexposed white fields where the subject partially disappears

## 10. Controlled Surrealism

Surreal elements should emerge from the composition, not be sprinkled on top.

Good:
- the character's reflection is slightly delayed
- doors repeat at impossible scales
- birds form the same directional rhythm as the character's hair
- a staircase continues into a blank white field
- flowers grow only along the path where someone walked

Weak:
- random glowing butterflies
- random magic particles
- random floating crystals
- random celestial symbols

One coherent impossibility is stronger than many unrelated fantasy effects.

## 11. Reference Calibration: What the Supplied Examples Demonstrate

The supplied reference set repeatedly uses three high-value strategies:

### Architectural framing
Large ornate frames, windows, mirrors, or panels divide the image and create multiple visual planes. The character is one element inside this geometry, not a sticker placed on top.

### Organic enclosure
Hair, flowers, cloth, branches, birds, or other flowing forms can wrap around the character and create a dense visual pocket surrounded by quiet space.

### Extreme environmental scale
A very small, still character can become emotionally powerful when placed against an enormous sea, sky, wall, road, or empty architectural field. The empty area is part of the subject.

These are structural strategies, not a request to copy any one image or artist.

## 12. Anti-Pattern: “Pretty Background Syndrome”

If a generated illustration feels weak, check these failure modes first:

- character centered with a generic scenic background
- environment has no interaction with the character
- decorative objects are numerous but causally meaningless
- every area has equal detail
- three or more unrelated atmosphere motifs
- magical particles used to compensate for weak composition
- symmetrical framing with no narrative reason
- costume detail is high but the image has no visual thesis
- lighting is globally beautiful but has no source or hierarchy
- character is fully visible and perfectly posed like a plate illustration

### Replacement move
Do not simply delete detail. Replace it with one structural idea:

`generic garden → one abandoned chair + long diagonal aisle`

`floating magic particles → one repeated reflection or shadow behavior`

`ornamental cathedral → one enormous doorway creating a light/dark split`

`character standing in scenery → character partially hidden by the environment`

## 13. Illustration Brief Extension

When `illustration-direction` is active, extend the blueprint with:

```text
**画面命题**：一句话说明这张图最核心的视觉想法。
**视觉母题**：一个反复出现并承担构图作用的形状/物件/材质。
**主构图形**：主色块 / 对比块 / 留白块。
**人物—环境关系**：人物被框住 / 被遮挡 / 被反射 / 被缩小 / 与环境共同运动 / 其他。
**遮挡计划**：哪些前景或结构会主动切入人物轮廓。
**视线动线**：观众从哪里进入画面，经过什么，最后落到哪里。
**尺度策略**：人物占比，以及为什么。
**色块策略**：3–4 个主要色彩质量，不先堆局部颜色。
**叙事残留**：一个能证明“之前发生过什么”的具体痕迹。
**唯一怪点**：一个超现实或不寻常的视觉事实。
**删减**：为了保持命题清晰，明确不加入的常见装饰元素。
```

## 14. Final Test

Before passing the blueprint to a prompt adapter, ask:

- Can the image be described in one visual sentence?
- Is there a dominant shape or spatial structure?
- Does the environment perform a compositional action?
- Is there one visual motif rather than a pile of motifs?
- Is the character's scale intentional?
- Is at least one part of the character allowed to be occluded or merged with the environment when appropriate?
- Does the negative space have a job?
- Can the eye path be described from entrance to focal point?
- Is there exactly one strong narrative residue?
- Is the surreal element coherent rather than decorative?
- If all particles and decorative effects are removed, does the composition still work?

If the answer to the last question is no, the image is not designed yet.
